Activity 1 Q1 - Explain why it is important to store and organise your files efficiently. (2-3 sentences)
Efficient file storage and organisation are critical in research because they enable team members to quickly locate, access, and understand the purpose of each file without confusion or duplication of effort. Well-organised storage also reduces the risk of accidentally overwriting, misplacing, or losing important data, particularly in large, multi-format projects involving literature, survey data, interview transcripts, scripts, and reports. Furthermore, a clear structure supports research integrity and reproducibility, as other researchers or auditors can trace and verify how data was collected, processed, and analysed.

Activity 1 Q2 - Explain your methods for storing the different types of data in the scenario. Specify which data types may require special access controls. (2-3 sentences)
For this research project, different data types warrant different storage approaches: literature review documents and draft reports are well-suited to a cloud-based version-controlled repository for easy sharing and change tracking, while raw quantitative data (CSV/Excel files) and analysis scripts (Python) should also reside in the repository with clear folder separation. Qualitative data, particularly interview transcripts, consent forms, and audio/video recordings, requires special access controls due to the sensitive and personally identifiable nature of participant information; these files should be stored in a restricted-access environment rather than a public or broadly shared repository. Photos and media files similarly require controlled access if they contain identifiable participant images, and all sensitive data should be encrypted at rest and accessible only to authorised team members.

Activity 2 Q1 - Why is it important to have a good naming convention? 
A consistent and descriptive file naming convention is essential because it allows anyone on the research team, including future collaborators unfamiliar with the project to immediately understand the content, version, and context of a file without having to open it. Good naming conventions prevent confusion caused by generic or ambiguous names, which become increasingly problematic as a project grows in scale and involves multiple contributors. They also facilitate automated processing and sorting of files by scripts or tools, and support long-term data management by making files discoverable and interpretable even years after the project concludes, a key requirement for research reproducibility and data archiving standards.
